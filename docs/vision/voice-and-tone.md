# Voice and Tone

How this design system sounds across the product. **Voice** is consistent personality; **tone** adapts to context.

## Voice (consistent)

Define your brand personality in a few adjectives. Example starter (customize for your product):

| Trait | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Clear** | Plain words, no jargon unless the audience expects it |
| **Helpful** | Orient the user; say what to do next |
| **Calm** | Avoid alarmist language unless the situation is urgent |
| **Direct** | Short sentences; active voice |

## Tone by context

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| **Success** | Warm, brief | "Your changes were saved." |
| **Error** | Calm, specific | "We couldn't save your changes. Check your connection and try again." |
| **Empty state** | Encouraging | "No projects yet. Create one to get started." |
| **Destructive confirm** | Serious, neutral | "Delete this project? This can't be undone." |
| **Loading** | Neutral | "Saving…" (not "Please wait!!!") |

## Capitalization

- **Sentence case** for headings, buttons, and labels: "Create account"
- **Title case** only if your brand guidelines require it — pick one rule and apply everywhere
- **ALL CAPS** — avoid except for small badges or acronyms

## Words to avoid

- Vague errors: "Something went wrong", "Error 500"
- Blame: "You entered invalid data" → prefer "Enter a valid email address"
- Technical jargon in user-facing copy unless the audience is technical
